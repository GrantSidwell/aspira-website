**Subject:** Appointment to appraise and rehabilitate the Cattle Market drum entrance building and pedestrian subway, Wheatfield Way, Kingston upon Thames

## 1. Introduction and appointment

We are appointing Aspira to tell us what condition the Cattle Market drum building and its pedestrian subway are in, and how to bring them back into safe, attractive public use. We are the preferred bidder for the Royal Borough of Kingston's disposal of the Cattle Market Car Park, which the Council's Corporate and Resources Committee approved on 20 March 2025 on a Subject to Planning basis.

Our residential-led scheme will put new homes on the car park site. Most of those residents will walk into Kingston town centre, and the shortest route is through the drum and under Wheatfield Way (A307). Today that route is derelict. We want it to be the front door of our development, not a reason for people to avoid it.

We need independent building surveying and conservation advice on these structures only. The new-build homes, the retained car parking and the wider public realm sit with our own design team.

## 2. Site and context

The car park site is 4,435 m² in Kingston town centre, built in 1985 with 600 spaces over a surface deck and two basement levels. The Council closed both basement levels to the public on 10 March 2020 at the police's request after repeated antisocial behaviour, and they remain closed. Council surveys found the car park deteriorating, with inadequate ventilation and sprinkler systems.

The points from the Council's disposal report that shape this commission are:

- **New homes.** The Council's planning team estimated the site could take around 80 to 175 homes. Our scheme is residential-led with ground-floor town-centre uses. We are planning within that range.
- **More people on foot.** The site has a PTAL of 5 to 6a and is a 320 m walk from Kingston station. The Council expects the disposal to cut car use and encourage walking and public transport, so pedestrian routes from the site carry more weight than before.
- **Neighbours.** The new Leisure Centre next door got planning permission in February 2025. The scheme must keep 150 public car parking spaces to serve it. A TfL bus terminus is next to the site, and TfL owns part of the land over the basements.
- **Connectivity.** At pre-application (5 December 2023), the planning authority asked for better links, including a new north–south route between the Museum, Library, Leisure Centre and the town centre.
- **Heritage and townscape.** Any scheme must respect the wider conservation area, which includes the Grade II listed library and museum building.
- **Design review and timing.** Our scheme goes through pre-application and a Design Review Panel. The Council expects a planning application within two years of Heads of Terms (April 2025), so by about April 2027.
- **Sustainability.** The scheme must meet the GLA's sustainability standards and support Theme 1 (Efficient Homes and Buildings) of the Council's Climate Action Plan.

Source: Royal Borough of Kingston upon Thames, Corporate and Resources Committee, *Disposal of the Cattle Market Car Park*, 20 March 2025.

## 4. Our objectives

Our goal is a route our residents and the public choose to use, day and night, from the new homes to the town centre. To get there, the rehabilitated buildings and subway must be:

1. **Safe and secure.** Well lit, with clear sightlines, good natural surveillance and no hidden corners, so the antisocial behaviour that closed the car park basements does not move here.
2. **Accessible to everyone.** Step-free through working lifts that serve both the subway and the 150 retained car park spaces, with a stair that is as safe and easy to use as the existing structure allows.
3. **Sound and weathertight.** Defects found, causes diagnosed and repaired properly, not patched.
4. **Respectful of their character.** The drum, its lantern and its roofline are a recognisable landmark on Wheatfield Way. The drum is not listed, but it was built with the car park in 1985 and sits within the Fairfield Conservation Area. We want its character kept and enhanced in a way that suits the conservation area.
5. **Low carbon and cheap to run.** Lower energy use and embodied carbon, and resilience to heavier rainfall and overheating, in line with the GLA and Council standards in section 2.
6. **Useful.** The small office should come back into active use that puts people on the route. We have not decided what that use should be. Part of your commission is to suggest how best to use the space.
7. **Maintainable.** We will own and maintain the structures once the works are complete, so we need a realistic maintenance regime we can budget for.

## 5. Scope of services

We need one report that tells us what is wrong, what to do about it, in what order, and why. It must cover the following.

| Report section | What we need from Aspira |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Client brief and context | Your reading of this brief. The historical, architectural, social, economic and environmental context of the drum and subway, their heritage significance, and the rehabilitation priorities that follow from it. |
| 2. Inspection and pathology | A systematic visual inspection of all three structures, with method, date, weather, access and limitations recorded. A schedule of condition and a reasoned diagnosis of the cause of each significant defect, with its risk and remedy. |
| 3. Existing building information | Measured existing floor plans and elevations, with scale bar, north point and annotation, plus a photographic record of condition. |
| 4. Rehabilitation and adaptation | Why intervention is needed, the options you considered, and your recommended interventions, tied to the defects and our objectives. Proposed plans, elevations and perspectives showing changes to layout, fabric, access and fit-out. |
| 5. Sustainability and carbon | Three solutions for energy efficiency, climate resilience and carbon reduction, including any renewable or low-carbon technology that suits the buildings. Compare short-term benefits with long-term implications. |
| 6. Schedule of works | A prioritised schedule: immediate (make safe), short term and long term repair, maintenance and rehabilitation. |
| 7. Legal and regulatory | What consents and compliance we face: planning, works in the Fairfield Conservation Area, Building Regulations (access, fire, safety) and environmental matters. Highways and TfL consents for the subway if relevant. |
| 8. Conclusions and recommendations | A clear, evidence-based recommendation we can take to our board and the Design Review Panel. |

We also want an executive summary at the front, and the schedule of condition, defect table and drawings in appendices. A 3D model we and the Council can explore would be welcome.
